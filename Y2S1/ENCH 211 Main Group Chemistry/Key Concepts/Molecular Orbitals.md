Molecular orbitals are the result of combining [[Atomic Orbitals|atomic orbitals]]. The resulting shape of the molecular orbitals depends on the shape of the [[Atomic Orbitals|contributing orbitals]], as well as the bonding type that is combining them, either [[Pi Bonding|π]] or [[Sigma Bonding|σ]] bonds.

The introduction to molecular orbitals begins by analyzing the [[Chemical Bonding|bond]] formed between two atoms. A molecular orbital diagram could be used to determine the [[Bond Order|bond order]] between these atoms. Looking at the diagram below, you can see that the atoms contribute their available electrons from their [[Atomic Orbitals|atomic orbitals]] into the molecular orbitals.

![[MODiagram.jpg]]

The [[Free Energy|energy]] of the orbitals increases through the y-axis, and as the [[Atomic Orbitals|atomic orbitals]] fill the lowest [[Free Energy|energy]] molecular orbitals first, we get [[Chemical Bonding|bonding orbitals]] from that [[Free Energy|energy]] difference. The molecular orbitals that have a higher [[Free Energy|energy]] than the individual [[Atomic Orbitals|atomic orbitals]] become [[Chemical Anti-Bonding|anti-bonding]] orbitals, denoted as either [[Sigma Anti-Bonding|σ*]] or [[Pi Anti-Bonding|π*,]] as it is [[Free Energy|energetically favorable]] for the atoms to separate. Due to that increased [[Free Energy|energy]], [[Chemical Bonding|bonding]] is a result of the tipped balance between [[Chemical Bonding|bonding]] and [[Chemical Anti-Bonding|anti-bonding]] orbitals.

More [[Advanced Molecular Orbitals|advanced]] methods of analyzing molecular orbitals require an understanding of [[Point Groups|point groups]] and their corresponding [[Mulliken Symbols|Mulliken symbols]], and [[Ligand-Group Orbitals|ligand-group orbitals]].
