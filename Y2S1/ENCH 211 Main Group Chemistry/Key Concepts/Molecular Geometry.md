The geometry of a [[Chemical Bonding|bond]] is determined by looking at the shape of the [[Molecular Orbitals|overlapping orbitals]] between atoms, forming the [[Chemical Bonding|chemical bonds]]. Between [[Orbital Hybridization|hybridization]] and the repulsion of each negatively charged [[Atomic Orbitals|orbital]], we get a shape that maximizes the distance between orbitals. [[VSEPR Theory|VSEPR theory]] can be used as a quick reference for [[Chemical Bonding|bond]] geometry, while [[Orbital Hybridization|orbital hybridization]] explains the phenomena behind it.

Looking at the geometry of a molecule requires analysis of both the typical geometries of the individual atoms, as well as how the rest of the molecule affects the structure. [[Molecular Strain|Molecular strain]] causes atomic geometries that have [[Chemical Potential|higher energy]], requiring smaller amounts of added energy to get past [[Transition Energy|energy barriers]], increasing the reactivity of strained molecules.

Some physical characteristics can also be affected by the molecular geometry, such as [[Polarity|polarity]], and [[Phase Changes|melting and boiling]] temperature. A net [[Polarity|dipole moment]] is caused by [[Chemical Bonding]] arranged in a manner such that the sum of all individual [[Polarity|dipole moments]] does not equal zero. [[Phase Changes|Melting and boiling]] temperature are also affected by the geometry as well ordered molecules have more sites that can easily exert [[Intermolecular Forces|intermolecular forces]] on neighbouring molecules. This causes the well ordered molecules to have higher [[Phase Changes|melting and boiling]] temperatures.

[[Steric Hindrance|Steric hindrance]] can also change how [[Chemical Bonding|bond rotation]] occurs, and plays a role in determining these physical characteristics. As an example, with a long [[Hydrocarbons|hydrocarbon chain]], we have a well ordered molecule that can exhibit [[Intermolecular Forces|intermolecular forces]] at many spots through the chain on other well ordered [[Hydrocarbons|hydrocarbon chains]]. The [[Steric Hindrance|steric hindrance]] from introducing two [[Bulky Groups|bulky groups]] on nearby carbons causes the [[Chemical Bonding|rotating bonds]] to arrange in such a way as to push these groups as far from each other as possible. This will introduce a bend in what was a nice, linear molecule, reducing the surface that can exert [[Intermolecular Forces|intermolecular forces]], as well as prohibiting what could have been arranged into a much better [[Geometric Tessellation|tessellation]] geometry.

Molecular geometry is also related to [[Isomers|isomers]], with [[Isomers|isomers]] having the same composition but separate geometries due to [[Chemical Bonding|bonds]] formed between different atoms in the structure. The change in geometry this causes can have minor to drastic effects on its reactivity and physical properties.
