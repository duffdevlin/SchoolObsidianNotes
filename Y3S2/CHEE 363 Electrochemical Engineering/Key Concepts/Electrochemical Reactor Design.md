1. Determine the species that you are attempting to [[Oxidation|reduce/oxidize]]. Finding the [[Standard Reduction Potentials|standard reduction potentials]], you can determine the actual [[Reduction Potential|reduction]] or [[Oxidation Potential|oxidation]] potentials for the conditions of each [[Half-Cells|half-cell]] using the [[Nernst Equation|Nernst equation]].
2. Once the real [[Half-Cells|half-cell]] potentials have been calculated, they can be added to give you the real cell potential, and whether it will be [[Galvanic Cells|galvanic]] or [[Electrolytic Cells|electrolytic]].
3. Calculate [[Electrolytic Mass Transfer|mass transfer limitations]] and determine the [[Overpotential Losses|overpotential losses]] to determine the minimum [[Operating Potential|operating potential]]. 
4. Perform a preliminary economic analysis of the process by calculating the [[Specific Energy Usage|specific energy usage]]. Using this compared to the respective value of product generated, determine if the process is [[Economic Feasibility|economically feasible]] from the perspective of [[Cost Estimation|operating costs]].
5. Depending on the desired process parameters balancing [[Safety Considerations|safety]], [[Reactor Operating Methods|operating method]], and design complexity, a selection between [[Monopolar Electrochemical Cells|monopolar]] and [[Bipolar Electrochemical Cells|bipolar cells]] should be made. 
6. Using [[Electrochemical Reactor Parameters|electrochemical characterization parameters]], determine whether the overall process is [[Economic Feasibility|feasible]], and whether changes such as multiple successive reactors may be needed to obtain the desired conversion.
