A principle defining the limit of precision at which the momentum and position of a particle may be known.
$$\Delta x\Delta p\ge\frac{\hbar}{2}$$
Given a particle that you are trying to measure, you must interact with it in some way. Using light we can "probe" a particle to make measurements, and as the light changes, we can make calculations to determine things like position and momentum.

When trying to determine the position of a particle, the "size", or wavelength $\lambda$, of the photon can be decreased in order to measure more accurately. When attempting to measure the momentum instead, using the equation $p=\frac{h}{\lambda}$, we can determine that increasing the wavelength is what narrows down the momentum.

Logically this follows that the product of these two quantities may have a minimum value, quantified by the inequality below.
$$\Delta x\Delta p \geq \frac{\hbar}{2}$$
