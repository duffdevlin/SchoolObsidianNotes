Wave-particle duality describes the phenomena of quanta to exhibit both properties of waves and particles. This behaviour is clearly exhibited in the case of light, as light can be split into component frequencies via a prism, but also can eject electrons from a surface like a particle.
Using this equivalence in the case of light, we can use the relativistic equation for energy of a particle with relation to momentum, ignoring the mass term given massless light, and equating it to the definition of momentum of light given a wavelength. This derivation can be shown below.
$$E=pc=\frac{hc}{\lambda}$$
$$p=\frac{h}{\lambda}$$
Further using the classical mechanics definition of momentum, we can solve for the wavelength of a particle given a mass.
$$mv=\frac{h}{\lambda}$$
$$\therefore \lambda=\frac{h}{mv}$$
Given that we can relate a wavelength with mass, we can assume that particles with mass can have a wavelength, given the combination of these three principles.
